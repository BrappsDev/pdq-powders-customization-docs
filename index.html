§01

Warehouse Floor App

A tablet-friendly, PIN-protected app for floor staff to run warehouse and production tasks without logging into full Odoo — every action is stamped with who did it.

Getting in

What it does
  • A touchscreen app called Warehouse App opens from the main Odoo menu (for staff in the warehouse group). The browser tab reads PDQ Warehouse.
How to use it
  • Tap the screen and enter your 4-digit PIN. Some actions (overrides) ask for a Warehouse Admin PIN instead.
Good to know
  • Each employee's PIN lives on their employee record (exactly 4 digits, auto-assigned). A Warehouse Admin toggle on the employee grants override rights.

Clock In / Clock Out

What it does
  • Clock onto a specific job — a manufacturing order, a vendor shipment, or as a driver — so time is tracked against the right work.
How to use it
  • Use Clock In / Clock Out. Manual entry, driver entry, and a force clock-out are available.
Good to know
  • Already clocked in somewhere? You'll get an "Already clocked in elsewhere" prompt.
  • Clock out of a production job with no recorded product activity and it warns "No product entries were found during your clocked time."

Clean Production — finishing an order from the floor

What it does
  • Lists the active manufacturing orders and lets you record what was consumed and complete the order right on the floor.
Good to know
  • On completion it posts a summary (components used, and where relevant the average stick weight) to the order and clocks out everyone on that job.

Moving & receiving stock

What it does
  • Inventory Transfer — move product into the Inventory location by scanning a package/lot and entering a quantity, as full packages or loose units. Over-limit moves need an admin PIN.
  • Vendor Replenishment — work incoming vendor/subcontract shipments: receive item by item, record discrepancies, complete the receipt, and assign a carrier / tracking number.

Waste, QC failures & leftover checks

What it does
  • Waste Processing — record scrapped material (quantity, lot, notes) from any location.
  • QC Failed — for a failed item, either scrap it or return it to the vendor (builds a return linked back to the original receipt / purchase order).
  • Inventory / Capsule check — split a leftover count into usable, unusable (scrapped), and R&D (moved to the R&D location).
Good to know
  • Every action writes a log note on the record — employee, product, quantity, lot, and time — so there's a full audit trail.
§02

Weighing & Verification

How sample stick weights drive powder consumption, the quality checks each sample must pass, and the verification gate before a run can proceed.

Sample weights drive powder used

What it does
  • During a run, staff weigh sample sticks in the floor app. The average sample weight × quantity produced is what determines how much component powder was actually used — leftover powder is calculated, not guessed.
How to use it
  • Each weight is checked against the order's target range. Out-of-range weights or failed checks show a plain message, e.g. "Sample weight 4.9 is below target minimum 5.0".
Good to know
  • Set Target Weight (Min) and Target Weight (Max) on the manufacturing order — max defaults to min + 0.2 g, and min can't exceed max.

Quality checks on every sample

What it does
  • Each sample can require up to five checks: Lot & Exp Printed Clearly & Correctly, UPC Printed Clearly & Correctly, Length & Ends Sealed, Artwork is Aligned, and Free of Defects.
Good to know
  • Which checks apply is set per finished-good lot (a Required QC Fields list); new lots start with a sensible default set.

The verification gate

What it does
  • Verification needs 10 passing rows in a row. If any row fails, the order is flagged Admin authorization required and all further app processing on that order is blocked until a Warehouse Admin enters their PIN.
Good to know
  • The admin override clears the flag and discards the in-progress verification rows; a clean run of 10 completes it.

The "Page 10" batch record

What it does
  • Prints the Finished Product Inspection Log (Primary Packaging) — the target range, lot format, then one row per sample: time, weight, a Pass/Fail pair for each required check, and the recorder's initials.
How to use it
  • On the manufacturing order, open the Sample Weights tab. View Sample Weights opens the recorded list; Print Page 10 Batch Record prints the log (it appears once a sample exists).
Heads upSample times on the printout are shown in Central Time.

Setup — which components are weighed

How to use it
  • On a bill of materials, each component line has a Use Sample Weights toggle — mark the components whose consumption comes from stick weights.
  • On a product category, a Require Sample Weights toggle auto-flags every component in that category as a stick powder.
  • On the manufacturing order, a Lot Format box describes how the lot/expiry prints on the stick.
Good to know
  • An order that uses sample weighing can't be planned until the Finished-Good Lot, Target Min, Target Max, and Lot Format are all filled — you'll get a message listing exactly what's missing.
§03

Batch Sizes & Splitting

Cap how large a manufacturing batch can get, and split an oversized order into several right-sized batches that each run independently.

Setting a max batch size

How to use it
  • On a product's Inventory/Operations area, enter a Max Batch Size (in the product's unit).
  • Under Customer Batch Sizes, add a customer + their own max — that customer's cap then applies at every level of their order.

Splitting to batch size

What it does
  • Splits an oversized manufacturing order into several orders of at most the max batch size — each a separate, independently-sourced batch (its own component transfer), not a backorder.
How to use it
  • On the manufacturing order, press Split to Batch Size (it appears when the order is over its cap). A Batch Siblings button shows the related batch orders.
Good to know
  • It splits the whole batch (every order feeding the same demand), so re-splitting after lowering the cap won't stack a second batch and double the quantity.

Planned or already-picked orders

What it does
  • If some of the orders being rebuilt are already scheduled, you're asked to unplan them first (a confirmation lists them). If any have started or finished production, the split is refused so finished work is never lost.
  • If a component transfer for the order was already completed, the split now reverses that transfer first — the staged components go back to stock so the rebuilt batch doesn't pull them twice.
Heads upThe confirmation warns that splitting deletes the linked transfers and component orders and rebuilds the batch. That's expected — it's how you get clean, independent batches.

Which "Split" to use

What it does
  • There are two different splits on a manufacturing order now, and they do different things:
  • Split (in the ⚙ Actions menu) — Odoo's built-in split. Divides the order's quantity into -001 / -002 orders that share one procurement group and one component transfer. It does not resize or rebuild any child manufacturing orders, and does not ask you to unplan anything downstream.
  • Split to Batch Size (header button) — only appears when the order is over its Max Batch Size cap. Rebuilds the whole batch into independent orders, each with its own component transfer, and walks through the unplan/guard checks above.
How to use it
  • Use native Split for an ad-hoc, arbitrary-quantity split that isn't about the max batch cap.
  • Use Split to Batch Size whenever the split is about staying under a product's batch cap, or when the order has child manufacturing orders / work orders that need to come along cleanly.
Heads upNative Split won't touch child MOs or ask to unplan their work orders — if the order you're splitting has downstream manufacturing orders depending on it, use Split to Batch Size instead, even if you're not near the batch cap.
§04

Batch Production Record

One click on a manufacturing order produces the editable Word weighing & blending batch sheet, with each ingredient broken into the exact packages to weigh, by lot.

Printing the record

What it does
  • Generates the Weighing & Blending Batch Production Record as an editable Word document from the order.
How to use it
  • On the manufacturing order, press Batch Production Record. The Word file downloads.
Good to know
  • It's only available once the component transfers are done (the picked lots drive the sheet). You get one record per split batch (Batch N of X).

How the ingredient amounts read

What it does
  • Each ingredient's demand is broken into full packages plus a partial, per lot — e.g. 4 @ 25 Kg and 1 @ 10.03 Kg — using the package size on the product.
Good to know
  • Amounts under 2 kg print in grams with a bold (GRAMS) flag; heavier amounts in kilograms. No thousands commas.
  • Lots and quantities come from the completed pick ticket. Actual weights, container numbers, and sign-offs are left blank to fill in by hand.

Setting a package size

How to use it
  • On the product, open the Batch Package tab and enter the package name, size, and unit. One per product.
Heads upThe package size feeds the breakdown, so a wrong size shows a wrong (but arithmetically correct) count — e.g. a 25 g package on a bulk ingredient prints hundreds of tiny packages. Keep package sizes accurate.
§05

Yield & BOM Report

After a run, reconcile every component — picked, produced, scrapped, returned to inventory — with the production yield, for one order or a whole sale order.

Running the report

How to use it
  • Press BOM Report on the manufacturing order (or on the sale order). In the popup, choose which orders and components to include, confirm the PO Number, then Generate Report — it downloads a spreadsheet.
Do this firstBefore you confirm, a prompt reminds you: enter all scrap for these products/lots first — the yield numbers depend on it.

What's in it

What it does
  • Columns include quantity picked, PO quantity, produced, scrapped, total returned to inventory, inventory remaining, and Production Yield (%), with per-product subtotals and a Sticks Ordered / Sticks Produced summary.
Good to know
  • Leftover powder is derived, not estimated: picked − used (from the sample-weight average) − scrapped = returned to inventory.
§06

Production Queue Planner

Keeps each work center's schedule tidy automatically — closing gaps and pushing nothing into the past — as jobs finish, cancel, move, or split.

What it does

What it does
  • When a work order is finished, cancelled, unscheduled, dragged to a new time, or split into a backorder, the other already-scheduled work orders at that work center shift automatically to fill the gap or make room, using the work center's working hours.
Good to know
  • It only rearranges work orders that already have a start/end time — it doesn't pull new, unscheduled jobs from the queue for you.
  • Jobs that don't fit in the working day spill to the next working day; steps of one order never run out of sequence; a small buffer keeps auto-scheduled jobs from starting in the past.

Turning it on

How to use it
  • On a work center (Manufacturing → Configuration → Work Centers), General Information tab, in Auto Queue Planning, tick Enable Auto Queue Planning.
  • Two settings then appear: Auto-Plan Start Buffer (min) (earliest a job may start from now — default 10) and Queue Ordering Method (Priority + FIFO, Deadline First, or Priority + Deadline).
Good to know
  • It's off by default and set per work center. The work center must have a Working Calendar — all capacity and timing come from that.
  • Every reshuffle posts a log note (on the work center, the order, and any backorder) describing which work orders moved.
§07

Quality Forms & Labels

Packing slip / bill of lading for outgoing shipments, incoming-inspection forms and a release label for receipts, and a saved signature — all as buttons on the transfers you already use.

Packing slip / bill of lading

What it does
  • Creates a Packing Slip / Bill of Lading PDF for an outgoing delivery.
How to use it
  • On a validated delivery, press Generate Packing Slip. In the popup: check Ship From / Ship To, enter the PO, choose who loaded and who counted, add special instructions, and for each line enter the number of packages and which pallet(s).
  • Sign the Shipper box (your saved signature pre-fills), have the driver sign Carrier, then Generate Packing Slip.
Good to know
  • The PDF is filed on the delivery, a note is posted to the delivery and its sales order, and it can't be generated twice. A Packing Slip smart button (on the delivery and the sale order) re-opens it. Entering packages for every line is required.

Incoming inspection forms & release label

What it does
  • For a receipt, generates the Incoming Material Inspection Form, the Component Inspection Report, and a 2x4 "PDQ Released" label. The headers auto-fill from the receipt; the inspection body is left blank for QC to complete by hand.
How to use it
  • When you validate an incoming receipt with tracked lots, the Inspection Forms popup opens (or press Generate Inspection Forms). Tick which documents to create, fill Manufacturer, supplier lot #, and expiration for each lot (these save back onto the lot), then Generate Forms.
  • Print with Print All Inspection Forms / Print All 2x4 Labels (or the smart buttons).
Good to know
  • Each document type is generated once per receipt.

Save your signature

How to use it
  • On your Preferences page, the PDQ Quality Forms section lets you draw and store a default signature, so it pre-fills the Shipper box on future packing slips.

On the delivery slip

Good to know
  • The standard delivery slip now also shows the Finished Good Lot and Lot Format.
§08

Out-of-Stock Alerts

Email the right people automatically the moment a product drops below the stock limit you set for its category.

What it does

What it does
  • When a product's available quantity (on-hand minus what's reserved) crosses below the limit set on its category, the chosen people get an email — subject ⚠ Out of Stock: Product (Location) — showing the product, SKU, category, location, current available, and the threshold, with a View Product in Odoo button.
Good to know
  • It fires once on the way down — not repeatedly, and not again when stock recovers — and it's email only (there's no in-app log that an alert was sent).

Setting it up

How to use it
  • Open a product category (Inventory → Configuration → Categories), find Out of Stock Notifications, set the OOS Threshold and pick the OOS Notification Recipients (Odoo users who have an email).
Good to know
  • The threshold defaults to 0 — alert only when stock goes negative. Set a positive number for an early safety-stock warning.
  • Settings on a parent category copy down to its sub-categories. Scope is per category — every product in it shares the same threshold and recipients.
§09

Customer Inventory Reports

Email a customer an Excel inventory availability report for just the products they buy — on demand, or automatically on a recurring schedule.

What the customer receives

What it does
  • An email with a 2-tab Excel workbook attached, covering only the products linked to that contact: a By Lot/Serial tab (part #, description, lot/serial, location, expiration date, qty on hand, qty available, unit of measure, and receipt date) and a By Part Number summary tab (part #, description, qty on hand, qty available, qty on order, MO Total Qty, Available After Production, unit of measure).
Good to know
  • Quantities are shown in one standard unit per product (kg for weight-tracked items, Units for count-tracked ones), so nobody has to guess grams vs. kg. Qty On Order only counts open purchase orders — not what PDQ is manufacturing internally.
  • MO Total Qty is the total of that part still needed by every open (not finished/cancelled) manufacturing order, regardless of whether it's actually been reserved yet. Available After Production is Qty On Hand − MO Total Qty + Qty On Order — PDQ's own version of a forecast, handy for spotting when a customer needs to ship more material soon.

Choosing which products a customer sees

How to use it
  • Open the customer's contact record, go to the Inventory Report tab, and press Manage Linked Products. This opens a searchable, filterable product list — tick the In Report box on any product to add it, untick to remove it. Select several rows at once to add or remove a batch in one go.
Good to know
  • The Linked Products count and a Linked to This Report filter (on the product list) make it easy to see exactly what a customer currently gets.

Sending to specific people instead of the main contact

How to use it
  • On the contact's Inventory Report tab, add people to Notify Contacts (the picker only offers that contact's own sub-contacts, or the contact itself). If set, the report goes to everyone on that list instead of the contact's own email — all on one shared email/thread.
Good to know
  • Leave it blank to keep sending to the contact's own email address, unchanged. Handy when a company is set up as one generic contact with specific people underneath it.

Sending a report

How to use it
  • One customer: on their contact record, Inventory Report tab, press Send Report Now.
  • Several customers at once: from the Contacts list, select multiple contacts, then choose Send Inventory Report Now from the actions menu (⚙).
Good to know
  • A confirmation appears once sending finishes. If a single contact can't be sent (no products linked, no valid email address, or one was already sent in the last 5 minutes), you'll see exactly why. Sending to several contacts at once shows how many went through, and — if any didn't — a popup groups the rest by reason with a way to open and fix them, then resend just that group.
  • The same contact can't be emailed twice within 5 minutes, so an accidental double-click or an overlapping scheduled send won't duplicate an email.
  • Every send is also logged on the contact's own message history, showing the actual email that went out — successes, failures, and a note if any Notify Contacts were skipped for having no valid email address.

Setting up a recurring schedule

How to use it
  • On the contact's Inventory Report tab, tick Send Scheduled Inventory Report, then choose a Frequency: Weekly or Every 2 Weeks (pick the day), Monthly (pick the day of the month), or Month End (the last calendar day of the month). Set the time of day the report should go out.
Good to know
  • The tab shows Last Sent and Next Scheduled Send so it's easy to confirm a schedule is active and see when the next one goes out.
  • Month End defaults to sending near the very end of the day, for month-close reporting.
§10

Lot Number Format

Ask Odoo for a lot number and it now builds PDQ's own format — a leading digit for what the item is, the date, and a counter that restarts each day.

Reading a lot number

What it does
  • A generated lot number is always nine digits — 926215001 reads as 9 finished good · 26 year 2026 · 215 the 215th day of the year · 001 the first lot of that day.
  • Raw materials, packaging, blends and other components lead with 5 instead of 9 — so 526215001 is the first component lot of the same day.
Good to know
  • The daily counter is shared across every product, so two lots created on the same day never carry the same number.
  • The counter continues from the highest number already issued that day, including lots that were numbered by hand — nothing gets reissued.

Getting a number

How to use it
  • On a manufacturing order, click the ⊞ button beside Lot/Serial Number. The number appears in PDQ format. The same button on the shop-floor tablet behaves identically.
Good to know
  • Typing a lot number yourself is unchanged. Nothing is reformatted, renumbered, or rejected — this only changes the number Odoo proposes when you ask it for one.
  • Material received on a purchase transfer still keeps the vendor's own lot code; there is no generated number there.
  • Adding to a number afterwards still uses it up — record 526215001-316001 and the next lot that day is 002, not another 001.
  • 999 lots per leading digit per day is the ceiling. Past that Odoo asks for a wider number rather than quietly repeating one.

Choosing 9 or 5 for a product

How to use it
  • The leading digit comes from the product's category. Open a category (Inventory → Configuration → Categories) and set Lot Number Prefix to either 9 - Finished good or 5 - Raw material / component.
  • Leave it empty and the category follows its parent, so only the categories where the answer actually changes need filling in.
Good to know
  • Set up today: Finished Goods and everything beneath it generates 9; everything else — Raw Materials, Packaging, Customer Supplied, PPE, Janitorial — generates 5.
  • A product with nothing set anywhere in its category tree is treated as a component (5), on the reasoning that a finished good starting with 5 gets spotted straight away while the reverse would not.
  • Bulk sticks and bulk blends sit under Finished Goods today, so they generate 9. Move a category, or set its prefix directly, if a group of items should number the other way.
§11

Changelog

What changed on each production update, newest first.

2026-08-03 · PENDING PRODUCTION PUSH

Customer Inventory Reports; PDQ lot number format; native Split restored

  • New — lot numbers in PDQ format. The ⊞ button beside Lot/Serial Number on a manufacturing order now generates numbers like 926215001 (leading digit for what the item is, then year, day of year, and a counter that restarts daily) instead of Odoo's own numbering, which reused the previous lot's date. Which leading digit a product gets is set per product category with Lot Number Prefix, inherited by sub-categories. Lots numbered by hand are untouched, and the counter continues past them rather than repeating one — see §10.
  • New: link a set of products to any customer/contact, then email them a 2-tab Excel inventory availability report (by lot/serial, and a by-part-number summary) — on demand for one or several contacts at once, or automatically on a Weekly, Every 2 Weeks, Monthly, or Month End schedule.
  • Products are managed from a searchable/filterable product list with an In Report toggle, supporting bulk add/remove.
  • Sending shows a confirmation, and groups any contacts that couldn't be sent by reason with a way to fix and resend them. The same contact can't be emailed twice within 5 minutes.
  • Notify Contacts: a report can now go to a list of specific sub-contacts under a company instead of the main contact's own email.
  • The By Part Number tab gained two columns: MO Total Qty (total still needed by open manufacturing orders, regardless of reservation) and Available After Production (Qty On Hand − MO Total Qty + Qty On Order). Quantities across the report are now shown in a standard unit (kg or Units) instead of each product's raw configured unit, and Qty On Order now reflects purchase orders only.
  • Every send is logged on the contact's message history with the real email content, so successes, failures, and skipped recipients are never silent.
  • Manufacturing orders: Odoo's native Split (⚙ Actions menu) is back, for ad-hoc arbitrary-quantity splits. It's separate from Split to Batch Size, which stays the right choice for anything with child manufacturing orders or work orders (see §03).
2026-07-20

Batch Production Record fixes & split pick reversal

  • Batch Production Record — package counts fixed. Ingredients whose recipe is in grams but whose product unit is Kg were printing counts about 1000× too high (e.g. 4,401 @ 25 Kg); they now read correctly, e.g. 4 @ 25 Kg + 1 @ 10.03 Kg. The batch total now reads in kg, not grams.
  • Cleaner amounts. Amounts auto-convert to kg or grams — under 2 kg shows grams with a bold (GRAMS) flag — with no thousands commas. Ingredient lots now come from the completed pick ticket, and table rows no longer duplicate across page breaks.
  • Splitting a picked batch. If a component transfer was already completed, splitting now reverses it first, so the staged material returns to stock and the rebuilt batch doesn't pull it twice.
